Potential Final Questions: Building the Levels
Proposed by Sefer İdacı for the final examination
Two questions for a closed-book exam, each designed for about 12 minutes and 25 points, on the fundamentals of the topologies talk: levels and device voltage with the NPC leg, and the states of a flying-capacitor leg. No derivations are asked; every part needs a short table, one number or one sentence. Solutions and a marking guide follow. All numbers differ from the lecture examples.
Question 1 · Levels, device voltage and the NPC legfundamentals25 pts

A converter has a DC link of Vdc = 1200 V. In an N-level leg each switch blocks Vdc/(N − 1). A switch may block at most 2/3 of its voltage rating; the available classes are 650 V, 1200 V, 1700 V and 3300 V.

  1. For N = 2, 3 and 5, give the voltage blocked by each switch and the smallest suitable device class. [9]
  2. For a 3-level NPC leg (switches S1–S4 from top to bottom, clamp diodes D5 and D6), list the switches that are on and the output voltage vaO in states P, O and N. [6]
  3. The leg is in state O. Which two devices carry the current when it flows out of the leg (ia > 0), and which two when it flows into the leg (ia < 0)? [6]
  4. Give one reason why the 3-level leg has lower switching loss than the 2-level leg at the same switching frequency, and one disadvantage of the 3-level leg. [4]
Question 2 · States of a flying-capacitor legfundamentals25 pts

The 3-level flying-capacitor leg in the figure has Vdc = 1000 V and its flying capacitor is held at Vfc = 500 V. Switches S1/S1′ and S2/S2′ are complementary pairs. The current flows out of the leg (ia > 0).

  1. For the four states S1 S2 = 11, 10, 01, 00, give the output voltage vaO and say whether the flying capacitor is charged, discharged or bypassed. [8]
  2. Which two states are redundant, and what is the choice between them used for? [5]
  3. Vfc is measured as 470 V, which is too low, and the next output level is 0 V. Which state do you apply? [4]
  4. The capacitor ripple is ΔV = Î / (2 fsw Cfc). Find Cfc for ΔV = 10 V at Î = 60 A and fsw = 20 kHz. [4]
  5. Why must Cfc be charged before the leg starts switching? [4]

(c) ia > 0: D5 and S2 (from the midpoint through the upper clamp diode). ia < 0: S3 and D6 (through the lower clamp diode into the midpoint). [6: 3 + 3]

(d) Lower switching loss: each commutation switches only Vdc/2, and the lower-voltage device is faster (less energy per switching event). Disadvantage, any one: twice the switches and gate drivers; two devices in series (more conduction loss); the midpoint voltage must be balanced; more states, harder control. [4: 2 + 2]

(b) 10 and 01: both give 0 V. One charges the flying capacitor and the other discharges it, so choosing between them keeps Vfc at its target without changing the output. [5: 2 + 3]

(c) The capacitor must be charged, and with ia > 0 that is state 10. [4]

(d) Cfc = Î/(2 fsw ΔV) = 60/(2 · 20 000 · 10) = 150 µF. [4]

(e) With an empty capacitor the switches no longer share the voltage: one switch would have to block the full Vdc = 1000 V instead of Vdc/2, and would fail. The output levels would also be wrong. [4]
