(b) Any two of each (slides 5 and 6).
Advantages: lower voltage on each device (lower-voltage, faster parts); smaller voltage steps (less dv/dt and EMI); smaller current ripple (smaller filter); lower switching loss.
Disadvantages: more switches and gate drivers; more devices in series (more conduction loss); capacitor voltages that must be kept balanced; more switching states (harder control).

Question 2 · Reading the circuits25 pts

(a) The current paths (ia > 0, from the midpoint O to the output):

A: NPC · O → D5 → S2 → a
B: T-type · O → S2 → diode of S3 → a
C: ANPC · O → diode of S5 → S2 → a

(b) They are redundant states: different switching states with the same output voltage. In D (state 10: S1 and S2′ on) the load current flows into the + terminal of Cfc and charges it; in E (state 01: S2 and S1′ on) it flows the other way and discharges it. Choosing between them keeps Vfc at Vdc/2.

Marking: (a) path 3, name 2 per leg; (b) “redundant” 4, charge / discharge 6.
