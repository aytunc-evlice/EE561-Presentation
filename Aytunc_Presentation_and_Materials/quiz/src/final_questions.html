EE561 Advanced Static Power Conversion · Multilevel Converters module
Potential Final Questions: Running the Levels
Proposed by M. Aytunç Evlice for the final examination
Three questions for a closed-book exam, each designed for about 12 minutes and 25 points, on the fundamentals of the talk: three-phase modulation with min-max injection, the midpoint of a three-level NPC converter, and the control of a grid-connected converter. The numbers are simple and need no calculator; most parts ask for a short derivation, a sketch or one or two sentences. Solutions and a marking guide follow on the later pages.
Question 1 · Three-phase modulation and min-max injectionfundamentals25 pts

A two-level three-phase inverter has Vdc = 800 V. Each leg follows its reference rx (−1 ≤ rx ≤ 1) by sine-triangle PWM, so that its average voltage measured from the DC-link midpoint O is rx·Vdc/2. At one instant the three references are

ra = 1.10,   rb = −0.55,   rc = −0.55.
  1. What happens to leg a at this instant with plain sine PWM, and what is this operation called? [4]
  2. Min-max injection adds r0 = −(max + min)/2 to all three references. Compute r0 and the three new references. Are they inside ±1? [6]
  3. Show that the average line voltage vab is the same with and without r0, and give its value. [5]
  4. Give the largest peak of the fundamental line-to-line voltage in the linear range (no overmodulation) for sine PWM and for min-max injection, in terms of Vdc and in volts. [6]
  5. Min-max injection gives the same switching pulses as which other modulation method? Explain in one sentence why. [4]
Question 2 · The midpoint of a three-level NPC converterfundamentals25 pts

A three-level NPC converter has a split DC link: C1 on top, C2 below, and the midpoint O between them. Each leg connects its output to P (+Vdc/2), O (0) or N (−Vdc/2). For r > 0 the leg switches between P and O with a carrier from 0 to 1; for r < 0 between O and N with a carrier from −1 to 0 (PD).

  1. Show that, within one switching period, a leg with reference r spends the fraction 1 − |r| of the time at O. [6]
  2. A leg at O draws its phase current from the midpoint. The average current drawn from O is therefore iO = Σ (1 − |rx|)·ix over the three phases. Using ia + ib + ic = 0, show that iO = −Σ |rx|·ix. [5]
  3. In normal operation the capacitor voltages ripple at 150 Hz on a 50 Hz grid. Explain why the ripple is at three times the grid frequency. [4]
  4. The states POO and ONN give the same line voltages. Explain why, what is different for the midpoint, and how a PI controller uses this to keep vC1 = vC2: what it measures, what its output is, and where that output is added. Why does the load not notice the controller? [10]
Question 3 · Controlling a grid-connected converterfundamentals25 pts

A grid-following converter (average voltage phasor V) feeds the grid (voltage phasor E) through an inductor L, so that V = E + jωL·I. A PV array feeds its DC link through a DC-DC stage.

  1. Sketch the phasor diagram for unity power factor (I in phase with E). Use it to state whether V leads or lags E, and whether |V| is larger or smaller than |E|. In general, what sets the active power and what sets the reactive power? [8]
  2. Draw the block diagram of the control: PLL, abc → dq of the currents, the two current PI controllers, the DC-voltage PI controller, dq → abc and the PWM. Label the signals between the blocks (θ, id, iq, id,ref, iq,ref, vd,ref, vq,ref, rabc). [10]
  3. The sun comes out and the PV power doubles. Describe, step by step, how the controllers react until the DC voltage is back at its reference. [7]

Solutions and marking guide

Solution 1 · Three-phase modulation and min-max injection25 pts

(a) ra = 1.10 is above the carrier peak: leg a stays at +Vdc/2 for the whole switching period and can only give 400 V instead of 440 V. This is overmodulation: the average is clipped and low-order harmonics appear. [2 + 2]

(b) max = 1.10, min = −0.55, so r0 = −(1.10 − 0.55)/2 = −0.275. New references: 0.825, −0.825, −0.825, all inside ±1. [2 for r0, 3 for the references, 1 for “inside”]

(c) ⟨vab⟩ = (ra + r0 − rb − r0)·Vdc/2 = (ra − rb)·Vdc/2: r0 cancels. Value: (1.10 + 0.55)·400 = (0.825 + 0.825)·400 = 660 V. [3 + 2]

(d) Sine PWM: phase peak Vdc/2, line-to-line peak √3·Vdc/2 ≈ 0.866·Vdc ≈ 693 V. Min-max: the phase references may reach 2/√3, so the line-to-line peak is Vdc = 800 V: the full DC link, 15 % more. [3 + 3]

(e) Space-vector PWM (centred, with symmetric switching). The min-max offset centres the three references, which splits the zero-vector time equally between NNN and PPP in every switching period, exactly as SVPWM does. [2 + 2]

Solution 2 · The midpoint of a three-level NPC converter25 pts

(a) For r > 0 the carrier runs linearly from 0 to 1 and spends equal time at every height. The leg is at P while the carrier is below r: fraction r; otherwise at O: fraction 1 − r. For r < 0 it is at N for |r| and at O for 1 − |r|. Both cases: time at O = 1 − |r|. (Same argument as d = (1 + r)/2 for a two-level leg.) [equal time per height 3, both signs 3]

(b) iO = Σ ix − Σ |rx|·ix = 0 − Σ |rx|·ix. [expansion 3, use of the current sum 2]

(c) |rx| and ix are the same waveforms in the three phases, each shifted by a third of a cycle. Their sum therefore repeats every third of a cycle: 3 × 50 Hz = 150 Hz. The midpoint current, and the capacitor voltages that integrate it, ripple at 150 Hz with zero average. [symmetry 2, 150 Hz 2]

(d) ONN is POO with every leg moved down one level: a common offset added to all three legs, so every line voltage is unchanged (+400 V, 0, −400 V). In POO legs b and c are at O and the current ia flows into O; in ONN leg a is at O and ia flows out of O: opposite effect on C1 and C2. The controller measures Δv = vC1 − vC2, drives it to zero with a PI, and its output is a small common offset r0 (with the sign of the power flow) added to all three references in the modulator. This shifts time between the upper and lower states (between “POO-like” and “ONN-like” states) and so moves charge into or out of O. The load does not notice because r0 is common to the three phases and cancels in the line voltages, like the min-max offset. [same line voltages 2, opposite midpoint current 2, measured Δv 2, output r0 added to all references 2, load does not see it 2]

Solution 3 · Controlling a grid-connected converter25 pts

(a) E on the real axis, I in phase with it, and jωLI perpendicular to I, pointing 90° ahead. V = E + jωLI closes the triangle: V leads E by a small angle δ and is slightly larger than E (the hypotenuse). In general the angle between V and E sets the active power (it flows from the leading to the lagging side) and the magnitude difference sets the reactive power (it flows from the higher to the lower voltage). [diagram 3, leads 1, larger 1, angle → P 1.5, magnitude → Q 1.5]

Unity power factor: V leads E and is slightly larger.

(b) Expected structure (any clear drawing with these blocks and signals):

eabc → PLL → θ
iabc → abc→dq (θ) → id, iq
(vdc,ref − vdc) → DC-voltage PI (× −1 for id > 0 into the grid) → id,ref;   iq,ref from the wanted reactive power (often 0)
(id,ref − id) → PI → vd,ref;   (iq,ref − iq) → PI → vq,ref
vd,ref, vq,ref → dq→abc (θ) → rabc → PWM (carriers) → gate signals

Marking: PLL and θ to both transforms 2; abc→dq of the currents 1; current PIs with references and vd,ref, vq,ref 3; DC-voltage PI giving id,ref 2; dq→abc and PWM 2. Feed-forward and decoupling terms not required.

(c) More power flows into the DC link than leaves it, so the DC capacitor charges and vdc rises above 800 V → the DC-voltage PI sees the error and raises id,ref → the fast current loop makes id follow (the converter voltage moves slightly further ahead of the grid) → more active power goes into the grid → when power out equals power in, vdc stops rising, and the integrator brings it back to 800 V with the new, larger id. iq is not affected. [DC voltage rises 2, voltage PI raises id,ref 2, current loop and more power out 2, back to 800 V by the integrator 1]
