Potential Final Questions: Modulation and Balancing
From the joint proposal by Sefer İdacı and M. Aytunç Evlice, for M. Aytunç Evlice’s talk
Two questions for a closed-book exam with the instructor’s cheat sheet, each designed for about 20 minutes and 25 points, on modulation and balancing: carrier-based modulation of a three-level NPC with a common offset, and a neutral-point balancing loop. Every question asks for a derivation, a numerical result and an engineering interpretation; full solutions and a marking guide follow on the later pages. All numbers differ from the lecture examples.
Question 1 · Carrier-based PWM of a three-level NPC, and its offsetmodulation25 pts

A three-phase 3-level NPC on an 800 V DC link uses phase-disposition (PD) carriers: an upper carrier between 0 and +1 and a lower carrier between −1 and 0, in phase. Within one carrier period the normalized references are r = (0.62, 0.18, −0.80) and the phase currents are i = (40, −10, −30) A. Pole voltages are measured from the midpoint O; iO is drawn from O.

  1. For each phase, give the two levels it switches between in this carrier period and the fraction of time spent at each. [6]
  2. Compute the average pole voltages ⟨vxO⟩ and the average line voltage ⟨vab⟩. [4]
  3. Compute the average midpoint current ⟨iO⟩ in this period. [5]
  4. Apply min-max injection. Give the offset r0, the new references, the new ⟨iO⟩ and the new ⟨vab⟩. What changed, and what did not? [6]
  5. Which offset would make ⟨iO⟩ = 0 in this period? Is it allowed, and what does this tell you about using min-max injection and neutral-point balancing together? [4]
Question 2 · Designing a neutral-point balancing loopbalancing and control25 pts

A 3-level NPC with carrier PWM has a split DC link of 2 × 3.3 mF; an ideal source holds vC1 + vC2 constant, so C dΔV/dt = iO with ΔV = vC1 − vC2 and iO drawn from the midpoint. The phase currents are balanced with Î = 40 A, and m = 0.9. A common offset r0 is added to all references.

  1. Starting from ⟨iO⟩ = Σx(1 − |rx + r0|) ix, derive ∂⟨iO⟩/∂r0 and its average over a fundamental period for rx = m cos θx, ix = Î cos(θx − φ). [6]
  2. With r0 = K · sign(P) · ΔV, derive the closed-loop time constant and choose K for τ = 20 ms at cos φ = 0.8. [6]
  3. With the same K, what is τ at cos φ = 0.2? What happens in rectifier mode if sign(P) is omitted? [5]
  4. At unity power factor the midpoint current contains a third harmonic of amplitude 8mÎ/(5π). Estimate the peak-to-peak ripple of ΔV. Can — and should — the balancing loop remove this ripple? [8]

Solutions and marking guide

Solution 1 · Carrier-based PWM of a three-level NPC25 pts

(a) With PD carriers a positive reference switches between P and O, a negative one between O and N; the time away from O is |r|. Phase a: P 62 %, O 38 %. Phase b: P 18 %, O 82 %. Phase c: N 80 %, O 20 %. [6]

(b) ⟨vxO⟩ = rx · 400 V: +248 V, +72 V, −320 V. ⟨vab⟩ = 248 − 72 = 176 V. [4]

(c) ⟨iO⟩ = Σ(1 − |rx|) ix = 0.38 · 40 + 0.82 · (−10) + 0.20 · (−30) = 15.2 − 8.2 − 6.0 = +1.0 A. [5]

(d) r0 = −(0.62 + (−0.80))/2 = +0.09; r′ = (0.71, 0.27, −0.71). ⟨iO⟩ = 0.29 · 40 + 0.73 · (−10) + 0.29 · (−30) = 11.6 − 7.3 − 8.7 = −4.4 A. ⟨vab⟩ = (0.71 − 0.27) · 400 = 176 V, unchanged. The common offset leaves every line voltage unchanged and moves the peak references away from ±1 (headroom), but it changes the midpoint current: min-max injection is not neutral for the midpoint. [6]

(e) While no reference changes sign, ⟨iO⟩(r0) = 1.0 − Σ sign(rx) ix · r0 = 1.0 − (40 − 10 + 30) r0 = 1.0 − 60 r0 (check: r0 = 0.09 gives −4.4 A). Zero at r0 ≈ +0.017: allowed, since all |rx + r0| stay below 1 and keep their sign. Min-max and balancing act through the same offset and share the same headroom: the balancing loop adds its correction on top of the min-max offset, and the sum must stay within the carrier band. [4]

Solution 2 · Neutral-point balancing loop25 pts

(a) For an offset small enough not to change the sign of any rx: ∂⟨iO⟩/∂r0 = −Σx sign(rx) ix. Since ⟨sign(cos θ) cos(θ − φ)⟩ = (2/π) cos φ for each phase, the period average is −(6/π) Î cos φ. [6]

(b) C dΔV/dt = −(6/π)Î cos φ · K sign(P) ΔV = −(6/π)Î|cos φ| K ΔV → τ = πC/(6ÎK|cos φ|). For τ = 20 ms: K = π · 3.3×10−3/(6 · 40 · 0.8 · 0.02) = 2.7 × 10−3 V−1 (an offset of 0.027 per 10 V of imbalance). [6]

(c) τ ∝ 1/|cos φ|: τ = 20 ms × 0.8/0.2 = 80 ms. Without sign(P), cos φ < 0 in rectifier mode makes the loop gain positive: ΔV grows exponentially until saturation and an overvoltage trip. [5]

(d) ÎO3 = 8 · 0.9 · 40/(5π) = 18.3 A at 150 Hz. Integrating, ΔVpp ≈ 2ÎO3/(3ωC) = 2 · 18.3/(3 · 314 · 3.3×10−3) ≈ 11.8 V (a numerical integration gives 11.9 V). The loop bandwidth is about 1/(2πτ) ≈ 8 Hz, far below 150 Hz, so it cannot remove the ripple — and it should not try: the ripple is inherent to carrier PWM with zero average, and chasing it would only inject large offsets. It is handled by sizing C (or by modified modulation). [8]
