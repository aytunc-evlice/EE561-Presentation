Question 1 · One switching period20 pts

(a) The carrier is a straight line between −1 and +1, so within one period it spends equal time at every height. The upper switch is on while the carrier is below r, i.e. while the carrier is in the band from −1 up to r. That band has height r − (−1) = 1 + r, out of the full height 2, so

d = ton / Ts = (1 + r) / 2.

Equivalent with the line equation: on the falling half the carrier is c(t) = 1 − 4t/Ts, which drops below r at t = (1 − r)Ts/4. The switch is off for that time at each end of the period, so ton = Ts − 2·(1 − r)Ts/4 = (1 + r)Ts/2. Checks: r = 1 → always on, r = 0 → half, r = −1 → never.

(b) The output is +Vdc/2 for the fraction d and −Vdc/2 for 1 − d:

⟨vaO⟩ = d·Vdc/2 − (1 − d)·Vdc/2 = (2d − 1)·Vdc/2 = r · Vdc/2.

(c) The local average follows ma sin ωt · Vdc/2, so the fundamental peak is ma·Vdc/2 = 400 V at ma = 1 (≈ 283 V rms phase). For ma > 1 (overmodulation) the reference leaves the carrier near its peaks: the switch stays on (or off) for whole periods, pulses are lost and the average becomes a clipped (flat-topped) sine. Low-order harmonics (3rd, 5th, 7th, …) appear (the 3rd cancels in the line voltages), and the fundamental no longer grows in proportion to ma; it approaches the square-wave limit 4/π · 400 ≈ 509 V only as ma → ∞. (Bonus mention: min-max injection reaches 2/√3 · 400 ≈ 462 V while staying linear.)

Marking: (a) idea of equal time per height or the line equation 5, result 3; (b) weighted average 4, result 2; (c) 400 V 2, overmodulation named 1, clipping / lost pulses 2, low-order harmonics or nonlinear growth 1.

Question 2 · Modulation and balancing15 pts
Part(a)(b)(c)
AnswerBCB

(a) B. A three-wire load only sees the line voltages, e.g. vab = vaO − vbO. An offset added to all three references moves all three leg voltages by the same amount and cancels in every difference (slide 8). It does not average to zero within a switching period (C): it changes slowly (at 150 Hz), so within one period it is practically constant.

(b) C. With POD the lower carrier is the mirror of the upper one, so when phase a goes to P, phase b goes to N at the same instant and vab jumps by the full 800 V. With PD the carriers have the same timing, the two legs switch at different instants and vab steps by 400 V: smaller ripple and lower line-voltage THD for the same fundamental (slide 13). PD does not change the number of switches or the fundamental, and it does not balance the midpoint by itself.

(c) B. ONN is POO with every leg moved down one level: a common offset, so the line voltages are identical (+400 V, 0, −400 V). The midpoint current is different: in POO legs b and c are at O and the current −(ib + ic) = ia flows into O; in ONN leg a is at O and ia flows out of O. One charges C1 and discharges C2, the other does the opposite; this is the free choice the midpoint controller uses (slides 16–20).

Marking: 5 points per correct choice; no negative marking.

Question 3 · Connecting to the grid15 pts

(a) Active power flows from the leading side to the lagging side, so from the converter into the grid. To send reactive power, change the size of the converter voltage: make it larger than the grid voltage (the higher-voltage side sends reactive power), keeping the angle near zero. In short: the angle sets P, the magnitude sets Q (slide 22).

(b) Using the grid-voltage angle θ from the PLL, the 50 Hz currents become constants in the dq frame (they turn together with the frame). A PI controller with its integrator drives the error to zero for a constant reference, but it always lags behind a sine. In dq the references are constants, so the steady-state error disappears; also id (in phase with the grid voltage) maps to active power and iq to reactive power, so P and Q can be set separately (slide 23).

(c) id,ref comes from the outer DC-voltage PI, which keeps the DC link at its reference (800 V): if the DC voltage rises, it asks for more id, sending more power to the grid. iq,ref is the reactive power we want to exchange with the grid, often zero (unity power factor).

Marking: (a) direction 2, magnitude for Q 3; (b) constants in dq 2, PI zero error for constants / lags a sine 3 (P/Q decoupling accepted for 1 of these); (c) DC-voltage PI 3, reactive power 2.
