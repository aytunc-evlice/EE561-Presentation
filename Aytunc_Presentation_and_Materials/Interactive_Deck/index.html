Basics · carrier-based PWM of one leg · read: Mohan, Undeland & Robbins, Ch. 8

Basics: sine-triangle PWM (SPWM)

Carrier c(t): a triangle between −1 and +1 at the switching frequency fc.

Reference (modulating signal) r(t) = ma sin ω1t: the wanted pole voltage, normalized to Vdc/2.

Comparator: S1 = 1 while r > c, so vaO = ±Vdc/2. The pulse width follows the reference.

Duty cycle in one carrier period (r ≈ constant), by similar triangles:

d = (1 + r)/2
⟨vaO⟩ = d · Vdc/2 + (1 − d)(−Vdc/2) = r · Vdc/2

The local average (blue) reproduces the reference. Everything else is harmonics near multiples of fc.

Question: 800 V link, r = 0.5 at this instant. What are the duty cycle and the average pole voltage? d = 0.75 and ⟨vaO⟩ = 0.5 · 400 V = +200 V.

2.5 min. Build it click by click: carrier → reference → comparator → duty cycle and local average. Then press P to run time: the zoom window follows the cursor and the pulse width breathes with the sine. Drag on the top plot to place the cursor by hand.

  • Push ma above 1: the reference leaves the carrier, pulses drop out and the local average clips at ±400 V (next slide).
  • Recap of the undergraduate leg; Part I used it on the two-level leg. The multilevel carrier lab generalizes the comparator: N − 1 carriers, count how many the reference exceeds.
Basics · ma, mf and the PWM spectrum · read: Mohan, Undeland & Robbins, Ch. 8

Basics: modulation index and frequency ratio

ma = V̂r / V̂c   amplitude modulation index
mf = fc / f1   frequency modulation ratio

Linear range, ma ≤ 1: the fundamental is exactly

V̂1 = ma · Vdc/2

Overmodulation, ma > 1: pulses drop out, V̂1 grows more slowly than ma, and low-order harmonics (3, 5, 7) appear. The limit is the square wave, V̂1 = (4/π) Vdc/2.

Harmonics sit in clusters at h = j mf ± k (j odd: k even; j even: k odd). Choose mf odd (no even harmonics) and a multiple of 3 (the carrier harmonic is common mode and cancels in vab).

Question: f1 = 50 Hz, mf = 15. Where is the first cluster, and what is left of it in vab? Around 750 Hz: h = 13, 15, 17. h = 15 is triplen and cancels in vab; 650 Hz and 850 Hz remain.

2.5 min. Sweep ma from 0.5 to 3: the red dot leaves the straight line at 1 and creeps up to 4/π. In the spectrum, nothing appears below the carrier cluster in the linear range; above 1, the 3rd, 5th and 7th appear.

  • mf 15 → 16: even harmonics appear (no half-wave symmetry). Then switch pole ↔ line-to-line: h = 15 disappears from vab.
  • Rule of thumb (Mohan): for small mf keep it an odd integer and a multiple of 3 (synchronous PWM); above about 21 asynchronous PWM is acceptable.
  • In three-phase, min-max injection extends the linear range to 2/√3 ≈ 1.155 (zero-sequence slide, three slides later).
Modulate

From a reference to gate signals

① Which level, when?

The modulator turns a continuous reference into a sequence of levels whose switching-period average equals the reference.

Example: 3L NPC, reference r = 0.6. Within one carrier period the leg sits in P for 60 % and in O for 40 % of the time → average 0.6 · Vdc/2 = 240 V on an 800 V link.

Carrier comparison and space vectors are two views of the same averaging.

② Which state for that level?

When several states give the same level, the choice is free for the output — so we spend it on something else: midpoint balance, Vfc, or losses.

From Part I: ANPC zero → OU or OL. FC zero → 10 (charges Cfc if ia > 0) or 01 (discharges). 5L-ANPC +200 V → “a” or “b”.

Rule: decide the level from the reference, then the state from the capacitor error and the current sign.

1.5 min. The two-stage picture is the key idea of Part II: separate the output decision (level) from the internal decision (state). For a 2-level leg the first stage is exactly the SPWM comparator of the last two slides.

Carrier lab: PD, POD, APOD, phase-shifted

4.5 min. Chain: compare reference with N−1 carriers → count how many it exceeds → that is the level k. Same m and fc/f1 as on the basics slides.

  • Question: 5-level, PD vs POD — which gives lower line-to-line THD? PD: its carrier-frequency harmonics are in phase in all three legs, so they cancel in vab. Show vaO vs vab.
  • For 3 levels POD and APOD are identical (only two carriers).
  • Phase-shifted (flying-capacitor cells): the first carrier cluster appears near (N−1)·fc.
  • m > 1 without injection: the reference clips → low-order harmonics. Turn on min-max → clean again up to m ≈ 1.155.
Modulate · zero-sequence injection · read: imperix, SPWM vs SVPWM

15 % more voltage for free: min-max injection

3.5 min. r0 = −[max(ra,rb,rc) + min(…)]/2 is added to all three phases. A three-wire load cannot see a common-mode voltage: line-to-line voltages are unchanged (table column).

Worked example (on screen): 650 V DC link, 400 VLL,rms grid → phase peak 326.6 V → m = 326.6/325 = 1.005. Sine PWM clips (max 398 VLL); with min-max the limit is 460 VLL.

Balance

Why capacitors drift

Split DC link (NPC, T-type, ANPC, 5L)

Vdc = vC1 + vC2    ΔV = vC1 − vC2
C dΔV/dt = iO    iO = Σx (1 − |rx|) ix

Ideal, balanced operation gives zero average iO but a ripple at 3f1. Device mismatch, leakage, transients and unbalanced loads integrate into drift.

Flying capacitor (FC, 5L-ANPC)

Cfc dVfc/dt = (S1 − S2) ia

Every redundant state moves charge. Alternating them keeps the average at zero but does not remove an existing error.

An off-target Vfc distorts the levels (P − Vfc ≠ 0) and overstresses the devices that block it.

Sum ≠ difference. The DC-voltage controller (or source) fixes vC1 + vC2. Only current through O — chosen by the modulator — moves ΔV. Same for Vfc: only the redundant-state choice moves it.

2 min. Both equations come straight from Part I’s switching functions. Balancing is a modulator job, not a DC-controller job.

Balance · analysis · read: Celanovic & Boroyevich, IEEE TPEL 2000

Why an offset can balance the midpoint — and when it cannot

Add a common offset r0 to all three references (line-to-line voltages unchanged):

iO = Σx (1 − |rx + r0|) ix

Sensitivity to a small offset:

∂iO/∂r0 = −Σx sign(rx) ix

Average over a period with rx = m cos θx, ix = I cos(θx − φ):

⟨sign(cos θ) cos(θ − φ)⟩ = (2/π) cos φ
⟨∂iO/∂r0⟩ = −(6/π) I cos φ

Close the loop with r0 = K · sign(P) · ΔV:

C dΔV/dt = −(6/π) I K |cos φ| ΔV
τ = πC / (6 I K |cos φ|)

C = 4.7 mF, I = 50 A, K = 0.0015 V−1 → τ ≈ 33 ms at unity PF; τ → ∞ at PF = 0.

  • The authority is proportional to active power: it is lost at low power factor.
  • Its sign follows the power direction: a fixed-sign gain is unstable in rectifier mode.
Question: check both claims in the simulation on the next slide.

3 min. Derive on the board; the simulation then confirms it (set φ = 90°, then φ = 180° with the naive sign).

Balancing the midpoint with an offset

5 min. 2 × 4.7 mF, 50 A peak. Starts at 420 / 380 V with the loop on. Speed ×0.1 so the 150 Hz ripple is visible.

  1. Loop off → ΔV stays offset (no natural restoring force). Add 1 A leakage → it drifts.
  2. Loop on → r0 moves, average iO goes negative, ΔV returns with τ ≈ 33 ms. PI removes the leakage offset.
  3. φ = 90° → authority ≈ 0 %: kick ΔV and watch it stay.
  4. φ = 180° with the naive sign → positive feedback → trip. With sign(P) it works. (Printed quiz, question 4.)
Balance · flying capacitor in operation

Flying capacitor: three ways to choose

Alternate 10/01: zero net charge per pair — but press Disturb: the error stays.
Always 10: Vfc follows the current — a swing at the fundamental frequency.
Balance: sign(ia)·(Vfc* − Vfc) picks the state — the error is gone within a few switching periods.

2.5 min. With an ideal current source, phase-shifted PWM has no restoring force; “natural balancing” in real converters comes from the load impedance at the switching frequency and is slow. Active selection is fast.

Control · modelling (same local averaging as the Control & Modelling module)

Averaged model: what the current controller sees

Average pole voltage of any of our legs, offset included:

⟨vxO⟩ = (rx + r0) Vdc/2,   x ∈ {a, b, c}

Balanced three-wire load: the floating neutral removes the common part, so r0 disappears:

⟨vxn⟩ = ⟨vxO⟩ − ⅓ Σy⟨vyO⟩ = rx Vdc/2

Filter L, R to the grid voltage e:

L di/dt = v − e − R i

In a frame rotating with the grid (amplitude-invariant Park, d aligned with e):

L did/dt = vd − ed − R id + ωL iq
L diq/dt = vq − eq − R iq − ωL id

Choose vd = ud + ed − ωL iq and vq = uq + eq + ωL id: each axis becomes L di/dt + R i = u, controlled by a PI with Kp = ωcL, Ki = ωcR.

To the controller, the multilevel leg is just a voltage source with the limit |v| ≤ Vdc/√3 (with min-max). The number of levels changes the ripple, not this model. Question: where does the ωL coupling come from? From differentiating in a rotating frame: the derivative of the rotation produces the cross terms.

Current control in dq: what each term does

Industry and research

Where multilevel converters live today

1500 V PV & storage

3L NPC/ANPC with 1200 V devices; hybrid Si/SiC ANPC modules put SiC where it switches and IGBTs where they conduct.

Medium-voltage drives

ABB ACS1000 (3L NPC, IGCT, since 1997); ABB ACS2000 (5L-ANPC, back-to-back, transformerless).

Wind turbines

Back-to-back 3L NPC/ANPC converters — both sides draw on the same midpoint, so balancing must account for both.

Research: wide band-gap

Flying-capacitor multilevel (FCML) converters with GaN for very high power density; SiC 3L ANPC for 800 V EV traction and chargers.

Research: control

Model-predictive control with balancing in the cost function; optimised pulse patterns demonstrated on 5L-ANPC MV drives; active thermal control through redundant states.

Open problems

Balancing at low power factor and in overmodulation; capacitor sizing vs. ripple; fault tolerance using redundant states; EMI of fast multilevel edges.

Summary

Three things to remember

1 · More levels change the steps. Clamp, float or combine: each device sees Vdc/(N−1); the output gets smaller steps and smaller harmonics.

2 · Redundancy is a control input. Several states give the same voltage — the choice balances capacitors, shares losses, or both.

3 · It only works as a system. Topology, modulation, balancing and current control have to be designed together — and checked in both power directions.
